I had had paracosms since before I knew what a "paracosm" was. Before then, I called them comics, projects, whatever adjacent words; they never felt right.

This post is prompted by learning about *The Age of Scorpius*. I came across a random YouTube recommendation on the topic, though I only skimmed through it because it was like two hours long. But here are all the things I know: *The Age of Scorpius* is a book written by Milo Winter which takes place in a fictional world called World of Guardian. Winter says he had been working on it since he was really young — for at least a decade. When it was published, it was really, really bad. He said he had editors, but the book was still riddled with bad writing that suggested either he didn't have an editor at all, or he didn't take any of the editor's advice. Also he hired a big team of artists to make absolutely gorgeous art based on the world.

That was all I needed:

- Started from when he was very young.
- Refused to take other people's advice to drastically change his story (this is not a negative sentiment, as I will address later).
- Hired a bunch of artists to visualize the characters / settings / etc.

Now, later I will address how whether something is a paracosm is completely up to the owner to decide, and also I'm not one to speculate on whether an online stranger has ever heard of a certain niche concept, but still I think this is one of the more blatant cases: *World of Guardian is a paracosm*. Had Winter known this, he might not have limited himself so much into publishing this one book. He could've made, for example, a website!! For all the character and worldbuilding information, as well as the art he commissioned! Had other people known this, they wouldn't have expected a writer-grade quality book! Really what could even prompt them to make hours-long videos ranting about this very simple situation. There are definitely worse authors out there.

Another example: Vivziepop with Hellaverse. This I feel is also very blatant. Started from a young age? Check. Has a hard time taking suggestions? Check.

For the "blatantness": it's "blatant," both for World of Guardian and for Hellaverse, because they were, from a literary standpoint, bad. But underneath the badness, the genuine passion and creativity is revealed, giving away how they are paracosms. There are many successful published paracosm works that aren't bad, too, which I'll touch on later. These are works that can be regarded as literary works in their own right, but are definitely also paracosms.

So begins my ramblings on what I think a paracosm is and isn't.
